# Common Symptoms and When to See a Vet

## This is general information, not a diagnosis
The notes below describe common, mild situations and general care. They are not
a substitute for a veterinarian. When in doubt, call a vet. Any symptom that is
severe, sudden, or getting worse deserves a professional opinion.

## Mild, monitor-at-home situations
A single episode of vomiting or soft stool in an otherwise bright, active pet
is often minor. Withhold food for a few hours, offer small amounts of water,
then reintroduce a small bland meal. If the pet stays playful and symptoms pass
within a day, home monitoring is usually reasonable.

## Call a vet soon
Contact a veterinarian within a day for: repeated vomiting or diarrhea lasting
more than 24 hours, not eating for more than a day, limping that does not
improve, an ear infection, or a small wound that looks red or swollen.

## Emergencies — seek care immediately
Go to a vet or emergency clinic right away for any of these:
- Difficulty breathing, choking, or collapse
- Suspected poisoning (ate chocolate, xylitol, medication, or a toxic plant)
- Repeated vomiting with a bloated, hard belly
- Seizures, sudden inability to stand, or unresponsiveness
- Heavy bleeding that will not stop
- Signs of heatstroke: heavy panting, drooling, bright red gums, collapse
- Straining to urinate and producing little or nothing, especially in male cats

These situations can become life-threatening quickly. Do not wait to see if
they improve, and do not rely on general information to treat them at home.
